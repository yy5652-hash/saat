The problem

"100% renewable" is an annual sum. Electricity is used by the hour.

A seaside hotel in Antalya holds certificates equal to its yearly use, half from a community solar plant and half from an Aegean wind farm. Over the year, the numbers balance. This is one day of it.

1
Issue

Every hour is checked before it can become a certificate

The meter export for each hour is checked against independent weather (ERA5) and the position of the sun by gradient-boosted quantile models trained on the plant's own history. Wind is checked against a turbine power curve driven by hub-height reanalysis wind. Hours outside the envelope are held. The rest go into one Merkle root per plant-day: one transaction for 24 hours.

2
Deliver & trade

Contracted shares are delivered; every surplus hour goes on the market

Each certificate is an ERC-1155 token tied to one plant and one hour. The plants deliver each consumer's contracted share, then list what is left, hour by hour. Evening and night hours are scarce, so they are priced higher.

3
AI day-ahead plan

Buy the hours you will be short, and move flexible load to the hours in surplus

The planner sees only what was known the day before: the weather forecast issued the previous day. Gradient boosting turns that forecast into tomorrow's certificates hour by hour. Laundry, pool pumps, water heating and shuttle charging (15% of the hotel's load) move into forecast surplus hours, and the planner buys the remaining shortfall from the cheapest listings.

4
Claim

Certificates are retired against metered load in the same hour, or not at all

The utility commits the consumer's 24 hourly readings as a Merkle root. The consumer proves each hour's load and burns certificates from that hour and zone. The contract caps every hour at its load and computes the hourly score on-chain, where anyone can check it.
